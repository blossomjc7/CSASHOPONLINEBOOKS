New Release

The Clock Heaven Refused to Hide

It hovered, unsuspended yet visible,
Not bound by wall, not tied to place.
Its face shone in the air,
Demanding witness.

No hand moved in haste,
Yet time spoke in certainty.
Heaven refused to conceal it,
As though the truth could not sleep.

I watched, unsettled,
As each number glimmered,
A code unspoken,
A summons to attention.

The clock did not plead.
It revealed, quietly,
And left me with the weight
Of what must be noticed.

Some things are too urgent
To hide, even from the eye.
Some time is sacred,
Even before we touch it.

— Written by Christine A.

Christine A.

Poet & Writer

Hey, I’m Christine A., and I’m so glad you’re here. I live in a place where the skies feel wide enough to hold your dreams and the streets hum with stories waiting to be noticed.

My life is filled with books, notebooks, and little moments that spark curiosity - a glimmer of insight in a dream, a question that won’t leave my mind, or a quiet pause in a busy day.

Poetry became my way of exploring those moments. Over the years, I’ve collected dreams, visions, and fleeting impressions, turning them into poems that speak to the heart and mind.

"I don’t write to impress or to sound clever - I write to connect, to reflect, and to share the small sparks of truth I notice in life."
— Christine A.

The Driving Force

What drives me is human curiosity and longing. We all, no matter where we come from, yearn for purpose and meaning. Poetry allows me to explore these longings in a way that’s honest, gentle, and sometimes playful. It lets me ask questions out loud, wrestle with ideas, celebrate small victories, and embrace doubt without fear. And the best part? You get to come along.

When I’m not writing, if permitted, you might find me wandering in nature, or concrete jungle, reading and meditating over a cup of warm chocolate about life’s oddities and wonders.

Travel inspires me - every place leaves a color on my mind, a feeling that finds its way into a poem. Music, light, laughter, and reflection all make their way into my work, shaping it in unexpected ways.

My poetry collections - Paths of the Pilgrim’s Heart, Quiet Lights, Questions at the Threshold, Eyes of the Invisible, and Simple Light - are designed to take readers on small journeys. Each one invites you to pause, reflect, and notice the beauty, wonder, and curiosity in your own life. Whether you are seeking adventure, insight, stillness, revelation, or plain truth, these poems offer a lens through which to see and feel life differently.

I hope these poems inspire you to notice your own journey and your own questions, to feel seen, and to connect with moments that might otherwise go unnoticed.

We may come from different places, speak different languages, and walk different paths, but our yearning for meaning and purpose is universal.

My wish is that as you read these poems, you feel a little less alone, a little more inspired, and a little closer to your own heart.

Welcome to my world - a place of curiosity, reflection, and shared humanity. I’m thrilled to walk this poetic path with you.

Poetic Vow

The Shared Journey

A soul set forth on winding streets,
Through alleys bright and shadows deep,
With every step, a heartbeat speaks,
A lantern held where secrets sleep.

I offer poems, not to command,
But to walk beside you, hand in hand.
Through questions, visions, and quiet lands,
A path unfolds where life expands.

Though our names differ, our hearts are same,
Seeking meaning, chasing flame.
Through verse we find our guiding light,
A shared journey in the quiet night.

Why These Poems Might Just Find You

Have you ever had a moment that hit you, but you didn’t know why? That’s how these poems started.

Welcome to Paths of the Soul, a 2026 poetry series that’s all about capturing life’s little flashes - dreams, impressions, and the quiet nudges that make us pause.

I’m Christine A., and over the years I’ve been collecting these sparks of insight. Some came from dreams, some from real-life moments that felt too deep to ignore. And slowly, they turned into poems. Each one is meant to be a short journey- sometimes playful, sometimes serious, but always honest.

Turning Life’s Moments into Poems

Have you ever noticed a tiny moment that stayed with you? A flicker of light, a quiet conversation, a thought that wouldn’t leave? That’s where most of my poems come from.

Over the years, I collected these flashes in journals: dreams, visions, and small insights, and slowly they became poems. But it wasn’t about just writing down what happened. It was about finding the meaning beneath the moment.

Each collection in Paths of the Soul reflects a different type of experience. Some are about journeys and discovery (Paths of the Pilgrim’s Heart), some about calm reflection and patience (Quiet Lights), some about wrestling with questions and doubt (Questions at the Threshold), some about visions and revelations (Eyes of the Invisible), and some about plain truths that hit home (Simple Light).

Writing these poems has been a journey in itself. Every poem is a little map - a way to explore life’s twists, pauses, and surprises. My goal is simple: help you see the beauty, humor, and insight in your own experiences.

Five Ways to See the World Through Poetry

Poetry isn’t just words on a page - it’s a way of seeing the world differently. With Paths of the Soul, I’ve explored five ways to experience life through poems:

  • Adventure & Allegory: Life as a journey (Paths of the Pilgrim’s Heart)
  • Quiet Reflection: Pauses, patience, stillness (Quiet Lights)
  • Wrestling with Questions: Thinking, doubting, discovering (Questions at the Threshold)
  • Vision & Revelation: Seeing beyond the surface (Eyes of the Invisible)
  • Plain Truth: Direct, clear, meaningful (Simple Light)

Each collection has 20 poems designed to make you pause, laugh, think, or reflect. Together, they form a kind of guide - not for where to go, but for how to see and feel life fully.

Reading them is like stepping into someone else’s mind for a few minutes, but discovering yourself along the way.
